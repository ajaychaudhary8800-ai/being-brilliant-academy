# Client Privacy Notice Guidance

This is guidance for institutions using Being Brilliant. It is not a substitute for the Client's own legal review.

The institution generally decides why student, parent and employee data is collected and therefore should maintain its own privacy notice/consent process.

## Minimum topics for the Client notice

The Client should clearly describe, as applicable:

1. **Who is collecting data** — institution legal/entity name and contact.
2. **What data is collected** — identity, contact, academic, attendance, fee, employment and platform-use data as relevant.
3. **Why it is processed** — admissions, education delivery, attendance, assessments, fee administration, communication, safety, employment and statutory administration.
4. **Who receives/processes it** — authorized institution users and service providers such as the Being Brilliant SaaS Provider.
5. **Children's data** — parental/lawful-guardian authorization and verification where legally required.
6. **Retention** — practical/legal retention periods or criteria.
7. **Security** — reasonable safeguards without making absolute-security claims.
8. **Rights/contact** — how a person can raise privacy requests/grievances as applicable.
9. **Consent withdrawal** — where processing depends on consent, explain how withdrawal works and its consequences.
10. **Third-party services** — messaging, payment or other providers where applicable.
11. **Changes** — how material notice changes will be communicated.

## Suggested processor disclosure

A Client may use language such as:

> We use authorized technology service providers to host and process institutional data on our behalf. This includes our education-management platform provider. These service providers process data under contractual and security obligations for the purposes instructed by us, subject to applicable law.

Adapt this to the Client's actual processing.

## DPDP timing

The DPDP framework has phased commencement. The institution should update its notice and consent/grievance mechanisms before the relevant operational provisions become applicable to its processing.

## Provider boundary

Being Brilliant should not represent that the platform itself makes the Client legally compliant. The platform can provide technical controls and workflows; the Client remains responsible for its own lawful purposes, notices, authorizations, retention decisions and institutional policies.
